Beyond the breaking point: the indicators now at risk

In a report published on 10 July 2026, UN Women found that at least one million women and girls had lost access to critical support since January 2025, based on responses from 855 women-led and women’s rights organisations in 52 crisis-affected countries. Some 120 million women and girls require humanitarian assistance and protection. Behind that warning stand four long-term series — labour-force participation, maternal health, girls’ schooling and representation in parliament — together with the aid flows that support them. Select a year between 1990 and 2025 to review each series. All values are retrieved directly from the World Bank.

      Data Sources & Methodology

      Indicators

      World Bank Indicators API, 1990–2025: SL.TLF.CACT.FE.ZS (labour-force participation rate, female) and SL.TLF.CACT.MA.ZS (male), SH.STA.MMRT (maternal mortality ratio), SE.SEC.CMPT.LO.FE.ZS (lower-secondary completion rate, female) and SG.GEN.PARL.ZS (seats held by women in national parliaments). Regional and income aggregates are excluded, leaving the 217 World Bank member economies; fewer can be drawn on the map because the boundary data set contains fewer countries. The number of economies with a value is shown in the panel above.

      Aid flows

      World Bank indicator DT.ODA.ODAT.CD, net official development assistance received by the world, 1990–2023, which is the latest year available in the series. Reductions in funding announced for 2025 and 2026 are not yet reflected in these data and are not shown.

      Context cited in the introduction

      UN Women, Beyond the breaking point: the continuing impact of funding cuts on women’s organizations in humanitarian settings, 10 July 2026, and the accompanying press release. The figures quoted in the introduction are taken from that report and are not recalculated here.

      Method

      Reporting years differ between indicators, so the map carries the latest value reported on or before the selected year and labels the year to which that value refers. Comparisons between a country and a global figure are made using the same reporting year. Rankings, callouts, the global series and all explanatory text are calculated from the API response when the page is loaded. No figure is hard-coded.

      Boundaries and licence

      Boundaries are taken from the open world.geo.json data set, retrieved at a fixed revision. World Bank data © World Bank, CC BY 4.0.
