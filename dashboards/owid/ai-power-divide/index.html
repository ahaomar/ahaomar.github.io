The AI power divide: who has electricity, and who supplies the power for AI

The electricity demand of artificial intelligence is entering the climate agenda: the 31st session of the Conference of the Parties to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (COP31) opens in Antalya, Türkiye, on 9 November 2026. The disparity beneath it is older. Data centres account for 4.9 per cent of electricity consumption in the United States, while approximately 657 million people — 8 per cent of the world’s population — have no access to electricity. The two series are presented together: access to electricity from 2000, and data-centre demand from 2020. All values are retrieved directly from the World Bank and Our World in Data.

      Data Sources & Methodology

      Data-centre electricity demand

      Our World in Data, based on the IEA Energy and AI analysis. Ten regions and economies are covered, with estimates for 2020, 2023, 2024 and 2025. The years 2021 and 2022 are not estimated and are shown as gaps.

      Electricity access and population

      World Bank Indicators API: EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS, access to electricity (per cent of population), 2000–2024, and SP.POP.TOTL, total population, for the same period. Regional and income aggregates are excluded, leaving the 217 World Bank member economies; 215 have a reported value, of which 171 can be drawn on the map because the boundary dataset contains fewer countries. The same economy counts are reported at the time of writing and may change as the World Bank revises its data.

      Method

      The population without electricity is derived at run time as (100 − access rate) × total population for the same year. Map shading, bars, rankings, callouts and all explanatory text are calculated from the API response when the page is loaded. No figure is hard-coded.

      Boundaries and licence

      Boundaries are taken from the open world.geo.json data set, retrieved at a fixed revision. World Bank data © World Bank, CC BY 4.0; Our World in Data © Our World in Data.
