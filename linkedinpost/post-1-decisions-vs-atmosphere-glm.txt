Every year since 1995, negotiators flew home from a COP with a new decision.
Every year since 1995, the CO₂ curve kept climbing anyway.

So I put the argument in one place: "Decisions vs. the Atmosphere" — all 31 conferences, Berlin 1995 to Antalya 2026, plotted directly onto the curve they were meant to bend.

How it works:

• Press Play and walk 31 summits in about a minute — host city, presidency, landmark outcomes, one stop at a time
• A verdict engine scores every summit from live data: CO₂ growth in the five years after versus the five years before
• Toggle annual CO₂, cumulative emissions, or global temperature anomaly — the curve, the budget, and the thermometer in one view

What the verdicts show:

Kyoto 1997 promised binding cuts. The growth rate barely flinched.

Paris 2015 is the first summit where the curve visibly bends. The first — not the last word.

And the stakes, measured: 23.5 Gt of CO₂ when COP1 opened in Berlin. 38.6 Gt today. Up 64% across three decades of climate diplomacy — with the planet now +1.5°C above pre-industrial levels.

One more thing. UNFCCC has no public API for its own decision record — the documents are bot-walled. So I curated all 31 summits from official records: hosts, presidencies, landmark decisions. The atmospheric data streams live on every page load from Our World in Data (Global Carbon Project, HadCRUT5).

One static page. No backend, no database. The atmosphere grades the homework.
