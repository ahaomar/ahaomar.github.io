LINKEDIN POST — "275 Years of Carbon: Who Emits, Who Emitted"
For: climate policy, climate justice, data-for-policy audiences, hiring managers
============================================================

In 1750, the entire world emitted 9,306 tonnes of CO₂. A single cargo ship emits more today.

I built an interactive dashboard that animates every year of the fossil age — 275 years of carbon, country by country.

"275 Years of Carbon: Who Emits, Who Emitted" turns the Global Carbon Project dataset (via Our World in Data) into a playable map:

• Press play and watch the Industrial Revolution ignite Britain, the US overtake it in 1888, the postwar boom explode across the map, and China overtake everyone in 2006
• Three views of the same history: Annual (who emits now), Cumulative since 1750 (who emitted it all — the historical responsibility view), and Per capita (who emits most per person)
• Click any country for its full 275-year curve, first recorded emission year, peak year, and decline since
• Auto-generated takeaways computed live from the data

The three views tell three different stories — and that's the point:

Annual map: China, the US and India dominate today's emissions.

Cumulative map: the map inverts. The United States has emitted 435 billion tonnes since 1750 — more than China, Russia and Germany combined. The UK had emitted 16.8 billion tonnes by 1900 alone — more than Spain, Türkiye or Belgium have emitted in their entire history.

Per-capita map: the Gulf states dominate. Qatar emits 41 tonnes per person per year — nearly 9× the world average.

Which view is "fair" is the question that divides every COP negotiation. My dashboard doesn't answer it — it lets you see all three and decide for yourself.

All data streams live from Our World in Data on every page load. One static HTML/CSS/JS page on GitHub Pages — no backend, no database.

Explore it here: https://ahaomar.github.io/dashboards/owid/carbon-275-years/index.html
