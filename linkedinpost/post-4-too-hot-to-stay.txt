LINKEDIN POST — "Too Hot to Stay: Climate Exposure × Displacement"
For: climate-migration policy, UN/IOM/UNHCR audiences, hiring managers
============================================================

Which countries are most exposed to climate extremes — and which are already seeing people leave?

I plotted 162 countries on a single risk map: climate exposure on one axis, disaster displacement on the other. The top-right corner is the crisis watchlist of tomorrow.

"Too Hot to Stay" is a quadrant-scatter dashboard built on the World Bank Indicators API:

• X-axis: share of population affected by droughts, floods and extreme temperatures
• Y-axis: share of population newly displaced by disasters, year by year (2015–2023)
• Four quadrants, four stories: the watchlist (high × high), disaster-driven shocks, exposed-but-not-yet-moving, and lower risk
• Click any country for its exposure score, displacement history, worst year on record, and a live-computed quadrant verdict

What the data shows:

The 2022 watchlist reads like a preview of tomorrow's emergencies: Bangladesh, Somalia, Ethiopia, Kenya, Malawi, Niger — all in the high-exposure × high-displacement quadrant.

The surprise is honest data at work: Pakistan's catastrophic 2022 floods displaced 8.2 million people — one of the largest displacements on record — yet the quadrant engine classifies it as "event-driven" rather than watchlist, because its chronic climate-exposure score is low. A single monsoon rewrote the map.

Somalia tops the 2023 watchlist: 2 million displaced, in a country where over 6% of the population is exposed to climate extremes every year.

This is the CLIMB concept from my platform architecture — climate and migration intelligence for decision-makers — built as a working product, not a slide. G7 delegations and UN teams don't need another PDF; they need the quadrant view, live, in one click.
