LINKEDIN POST — "Is Your City Hotter Than It Used to Be?"
For: general/professional audiences, climate communicators, hiring managers — the demo piece
============================================================

Global warming is +1.5°C. But nobody lives in "global."

I built a dashboard that answers the question everyone actually asks: what happened to MY city? Search any city on Earth — from Karachi to Reykjavík — and see 85 years of its local temperature story, straight from the Copernicus ERA5 archive.

"Is Your City Hotter Than It Used to Be?" gives you:

• The headline number: your city's warming between the 1940s and the last decade — computed live from daily data
• Then-vs-now temperature distributions: two histograms of daily means, showing the whole climate shifting right
• Month-by-month comparison: see which seasons warmed most — often summer shifts differently than winter
• Every year since 1940, plotted with a trend line
• All nine decades side by side, plus auto-generated takeaways comparing your city's warming to the global average

What I found building it:

Cities don't warm evenly. Karachi — my city — has warmed just +0.24°C since the 1940s, because the ocean next door buffers it. Landlocked and high-latitude cities routinely show +1.5 to +3°C. The same planet, wildly different local stories — that's exactly why "global average" fails to move people.

The whole pipeline is live: geocoding search (Open-Meteo Geocoding API), then 85 years of daily reanalysis data fetched and analysed in the browser in a couple of seconds. No API key. No backend. No database. One static page on GitHub Pages.

This is the piece I open when someone says climate data doesn't feel real. Type your hometown and watch.
