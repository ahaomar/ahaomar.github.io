In ten years, the world's refugee population nearly doubled.

It did not double quietly, and it did not double evenly. Syria grinds on for a decade. Afghanistan doubles after 2021. Ukraine goes from 28,000 refugees abroad in 2021 to 5.7 million a year later.

I built "Ten Years of Flight" to put those three timelines on one map — a decade of UNHCR refugee statistics (2015–2024), animated:

• Press Play and watch the decade unfold — crises emerge, peak, and fade across a world map
• Every country shown from both sides: who people flee (origin) and who shelters them (host)
• Click any country for its full decade — peak year, largest single-year jump, takeaways computed live from the data
• Top origins table for every year, 2015 → 2024

What the data says:

From 16.1 million refugees worldwide in 2015 to over 31 million in 2024.

The 2024 top three — Syria, Afghanistan, Ukraine — carry more than 16 million between them. Three crises, three very different clocks.

And the sharpest line on the map is the hosting one: the countries sheltering the most refugees are not the wealthiest. Iran and Türkiye top the list. Protection concentrates where the borders are, not where the budgets are.

No PDFs, no press releases. Every figure streams live from the UNHCR Population Statistics API on every page load. One static page — no backend, no database.

Refugee data should be explorable, not locked in reports.
